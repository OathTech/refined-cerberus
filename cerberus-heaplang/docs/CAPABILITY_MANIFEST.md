WHAT GREEN ESTABLISHES, EXACTLY. (1) The hand-maintained variant table below
(`variants` in the generator: the engine-SUCCESS shapes of every `Frag`
constructor, read off `Frag`, `Step` and the engine's memory-operation arms)
covers every constructor of `Frag` in the built environment and names no stale
constructor. (2) Every theorem a row names exists and is a theorem. (3) Every
RULE row's partial AND total rule, and every PARTIAL-ONLY row's rule, lies in
the proof-term dependency cone of at least one CONSUMER module — the modules
classified `positive-client` or `declared-smoke` in `scripts/module_classes.tsv`
— listed in the row. An UNDEMONSTRATED row instead checks that its named
side has no consumer and the other side does; both rules must exist.
A new consumer on the missing side requires reclassification to RULE.
(4) The module classification is complete and exact
(every package module classified; every classified module present; classes in
the vocabulary). (5) Every declaration the claim matrix `docs/CLAIMS.md` names
exists, and every declaration-shaped backticked span of every cell of a claim row
is a constant of the environment or a listed vocabulary word (planted).
GREEN DOES NOT ESTABLISH that the variant table is exhaustive over the
engine's success shapes (it is a reviewed reading, not a theorem), that a rule is
the strongest statement of its variant, or that a consumer's dependency on a rule
is the load-bearing step of its headline proof rather than incidental. A
NO-RULE or OUT-OF-SCOPE row is a stated absence, not coverage: programs
exercising those shapes are outside the logic (the reason and the deciding
record are in the row). Engine kills/UB/panics are not rows (not successes;
classified in Round.lean).
